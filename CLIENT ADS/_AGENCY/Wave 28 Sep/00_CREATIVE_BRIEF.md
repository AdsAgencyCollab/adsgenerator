## The voice, defined so it can be checked

Sabri Suby's register is not "punchy marketing copy". It has specific moves, and
copy either does them or it doesn't:

1. **Open on the reader's private pain, stated more precisely than they'd say it.**
   Not "struggling with ads" — "your last agency spent four months learning your
   category on your card."
2. **Name a villain.** Never the reader. The generalist agency, the freelancer who
   has never read the health policy, the retainer that bought a learning curve.
3. **Forensic specificity over superlative.** A real detail beats an adjective every
   time. "They wrote a claim. It got flagged. Reach died."
4. **Short sentences. Fragments. Line breaks that force a beat.**
5. **Qualify by exclusion.** "If you want someone to learn on your budget, we're the
   wrong call." Saying who it is *not* for is what makes it credible.
6. **Risk reversal at the close.** Give something away before asking.
7. **No corporate vocabulary.** No elevate, seamless, effortless, partner with,
   unlock, leverage, solutions.

**What we cannot do, and why it changes the writing:** the claim bank forbids
testimonials, prices, scarcity, countdowns, dates and any number outside L1–L12.
Suby leans on proof and urgency; we have neither. So the whole wave leans on the
other three levers instead — **villain, specificity, exclusion**. That is a
narrower instrument and it is the honest one.

**Formats this rules out:** trustpilot reviews, zero stars, testimonial, offer,
bundle, low-stock alert, we're sorry, countdown. All need a fabricated quote, a
price or a scarcity count. None are in the fifteen.

**Compliance carried through every asset:** no hands, no people, no syringes, no
needles, no numerals outside the claim bank, no platform logos, no real brand
marks, no em dashes.
