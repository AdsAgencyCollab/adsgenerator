## The surface rule predicted correctly, prospectively

Jobs 3, 6, 7 and 8 established the rule retrospectively. This job tested it in
advance: the brief put the 9:16 footer on the wall below the sign at 77%, which
the rule says will migrate to the frame edge, so it was moved onto the sign's
lower left instead. Result:

| job | footer on | briefed | landed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | bare desk | 77% | 92.8–95.4% |
| 9 | **the sign** | 68–70% | **72.1% and 70.3%** |

Same model, same ratio, same brief author, one week apart — the only change was the
surface. The footer held. **Five for five, and the first time it was used as a
prediction rather than an explanation.**

## The converse also holds, and it is the top problem

This brief puts the headline on bare wall *above* the sign, with no object up
there at all. It ran to the top edge harder than anything so far:

| | briefed | take 1 | take 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| headline first line | 15% | **5.8%** | **5.2%** |

Against job 7's 11.2% and job 8's 12.5%, both of which at least had a card or
sheet edge nearby. With nothing to sit on, type goes to the nearest frame edge —
top as readily as bottom. The rule is symmetric and should be stated that way:
**type on an object stays where it is put; type on bare ground runs to the nearest
edge of the frame.**

Neither 9:16 take is usable as-is: the headline is 8 to 9 points inside Meta's
Reels top band. The fix is the same one as the footer — put the headline on a
surface. Extend the sign upward to about 12% and print the headline on its face,
or add a second smaller plate above the directory.

1:1 takes are unaffected: feed has no UI overlay, and both footers at 91.1–94.8%
have nothing to collide with.
