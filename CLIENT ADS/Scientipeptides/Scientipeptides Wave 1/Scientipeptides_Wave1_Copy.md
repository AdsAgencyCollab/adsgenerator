## The insight this wave is built on

> *"I want to trust my supplier, but every vendor prints 99% on the label, and
> I have no way of telling a real COA from one they typed up themselves."*

Every long-running advertiser in this category has converged on the same move:
a **superlative about the seller** ("The Most Trusted Name in Research
Peptides") backed by a stack of vendor credentials. Zero compound claims. It is
both the compliance-survival strategy and the correct commercial read — the
buyer already knows what BPC-157 is; what they cannot determine is which seller
is real.

But the whole surviving cohort states its credentials **calmly**. Nobody uses
the category's actual tension. That gap is what this wave takes.

**The proposition:** *You don't have to trust Scientipeptides. Every lot is
tested by Freedom Diagnostics, and the report is searchable on the lab's own
system by accession number.* Third-party verification you can complete without
the vendor in the room. That is a genuinely different argument from "we are the
most trusted", and it is fully substantiated (S01, S10, S11).
